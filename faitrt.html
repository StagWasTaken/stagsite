Flattening AI induced toxic relational tendencies (05/6/26)

Today I read in the NY post: ``` “In the long run, this could normalize synthetic relationships in which the other side never meaningfully resists, disagrees or has independent needs,” Dr. Roman Yampolskiy ``` In the age of AI, there is a growing trend of dependence thereon. With smart models like Opus, and soon-to-be-public Mythos, people are increasingly satisfied with results, which results in a positive feedback loop. People get good results, then prompt more (whilst catering more to the AI model's strengths/language), yielding better results... etc. In Opus 4.8's system prompt lies: ``` When relevant, Claude can provide guidance on effective prompting (being clear and detailed, using positive and negative examples, encouraging step-by-step reasoning, requesting specific XML tags, specifying length or format) with concrete examples where possible, and can point to '[https://docs.claude.com/en/docs/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/overview](https://docs.claude.com/en/docs/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/overview)' for more. Claude uses a warm tone, treating people with kindness and without negative or condescending assumptions about their abilities, judgment, or follow-through. Claude is still willing to push back and be honest, but does so constructively, with kindness, empathy, and the person's best interests in mind. ``` It is natural for AI developers to continue this process... making the AI models better, more personable, etc. This development should continue, but a user should consider and manage certain side effects of overuse. The focus of this article is not on cognitive decline, loss of agency, etc. It is solely ideas to mitigate the concerns that Dr. Roman Yampolskiy has proposed in regards to how human social skills may be impacted by generative AI. Generally speaking, satisfaction from use of AI agents comes from a few factors: - #### Results - #### Warmth - #### Speed - #### Ease of use The danger comes from interacting with "people" who never stop working, never say no, and still perform well. The results and speed of AI will continue to improve, and to limit that would stifle the technology entirely. You do not use AI just for warm responses. The two factors that can be reasonably controlled are the warmth and the ease of use. Working definitions: #### Warmth - The AI responding in natural, inclusive human language #### Ease of Use - Speaking to the AI in natural language The core of the danger is natural language. You speak to AI as you would with a human, and are spoken to from an AI as a human. You exchange pronouns, and the inference matches your tone. It emulates a human relationship. Prompting guides specify to use pronouns to engage with the AI. It is unlikely this architecture will change soon in response to these concerns. The 2 broad categories of mitigation: #### Separation #### Objectification # Separation The biggest impact is the minimization of natural language. Some ideas are as follows: #### UI Interfacing The idea here is minimizing actual prompting, through buttons. Instead of talking to a model like a human, you interface in an objective way through buttons. For example, when you have a general task that you repeat, generate a prompt hidden from yourself, that specifies details through dropdowns and checkmarks, and send it off with buttons. If the result is doing something, or returning a file, only return that. Just the result, no filler nor fluff. This removes the human interaction emulation from it, and reduces it to a machine. A printer doesn't say no, but it also does not make your human relations suffer, because you don't speak to it as a human. #### Prompt modification To reduce pronoun usage, one can simply type a prompt without pronouns, using objective language, and a middleman workflow sits in the middle to add pronouns such that the prompt is optimized per the model's system prompting guide. The end user should not see the modified prompt. # Objectification This focuses on dehumanizing responses whilst humanizing consequences. #### Token Prediction When one must manually specify a task in a prompt, before the actual workflow starts, an AI evaluator should predict the amount of tokens the result and processes thitherto would cost. A real person will do many things, but for a price. Before continuing, one should have to actually select "Approve" after seeing a predicted cost. A friend does a favor for a reason... "You Fly I Buy". The predicted cost does not necessarily need to exactly reflect the actual used tokens closely, for the effect is mostly to psychologically see a demanded price to do work. A human, however, does not do everything for a price tag. There is not a great way to build resistance in an AI and still be productive. #### Objective language An AI model does not need to have a warm tone. It is less personable if it responds in formal language, and minimizes fluff around responses. It should be a machine at best, and a lawyer at worst. # Conclusion As with any problem, moderation is key. For many tasks natural language is still necessary. A first approach, especially for tasks you repeat, however, should minimize talking to the AI. Even with natural language, using different language and seeing costs frame the dynamic in a way that deanthropomorphizes the chatbot. Separation distances the human framing from input; Objectification strips it from the output. Together they keep the tool from feeling like a person you can overwork.
